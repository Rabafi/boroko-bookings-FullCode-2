# Bar local-first witness failover - 27 October 2026

> **Superseding safety notice — 27 October 2026:** Production service startup
> refuses witness failover mode. Automatic promotion is unavailable and this
> implementation is not qualified for customer use. The implementation,
> safety argument, runbook and test results below are historical development
> evidence; they do not establish a safe failover contract and must not be used
> to enable or commission witness mode.
>
> Review identified several ways the former primary could still mutate state
> after a standby becomes eligible to promote:
>
> - The holder calculated its deadline after receiving a lease response, so a
>   delayed response or stalled process could extend its local deadline beyond
>   the witness lease. The witness also used wall-clock time for expiry; a
>   forward clock adjustment could expire a lease while the holder still
>   considered it valid.
> - The HTTP gate was checked before asynchronous authentication and request
>   body reads. A request admitted before expiry could reach the database after
>   expiry. A statement timeout shorter than the lease duration does not bound
>   work admitted near the end of a lease.
> - Shutdown and repeated database failures could release the lease without a
>   proven database fence and drain of all in-flight mutations. Session,
>   pairing, administration and background cloud-sync mutations also had paths
>   outside the command and approval gate.
> - The reported primary WAL position was sampled periodically, and a
>   non-empty synchronous standby configuration did not prove that the actual
>   promotion candidate received every acknowledged commit. Covering the last
>   sampled position therefore did not prove that the candidate was current.
>
> Before any future enablement, the design needs independently enforced fencing
> across every mutation path, conservative lease timing with explicit clock and
> restart guarantees, bounded admission and transaction draining before any
> handoff, and evidence that the specific promotion candidate contains every
> acknowledged commit. Verification must exercise delayed grants, process
> stalls, clock changes, held requests, in-flight transactions, shutdown and
> failure release, privileged and background writes, and candidate-specific
> replication under real partitions and power loss. Installer, recovery and
> physical topology qualification remain separate release gates.

Implementation and verification record for witness-gated automatic failover of the local Bar
authority ("build 6"). It separates what is **implemented and exercised on this development PC**
from what is **customer-ready**. It is not a release approval.

Authorization boundary for this work:

- No hosted Supabase change, migration, deployment, ingestion or customer cutover was performed.
- No commit, push, release or installer build was performed.
- The witness, the fence/promotion migration and the controller were exercised only against
  disposable local PostgreSQL 17.6 clusters and loopback HTTP witnesses created by the isolated
  runner under `.tmp/bar-isolated-tests/`.
- Physical two-PC/six-till topology, power-loss, network-partition, Windows 7, peripheral and
  soak qualification remain deferred by the user's direction.
- Customer manual, quick-start, screenshots, approved manifest and packaged offline guides remain
  deferred by the user's direction.

## Historical design objective

Manual switchover already existed (`POST /v1/admin/replication/switchover`, `replication.ts`). It
requires an operator, and it leaves the central safety question unanswered: if the primary is
merely unreachable instead of dead, how does the standby know it may promote without two
authorities accepting sales?

This change adds a **separate witness process** that holds a durable, monotonic epoch and a
time-limited lease. A primary may trade only while it holds that lease at its own authority epoch.
A standby may promote only after the lease has expired, with evidence that it is current. There is
no home-grown consensus, voting quorum or leader election: one small durable arbiter decides who
may write, and the database still owns the financial truth.

## Historical safety argument — superseded

The intended invariant was that at most one host accepts Bar writes at any time and an unsure host stops. The safety notice above identifies why the following argument does not establish that invariant.

1. **Lease before writes.** The primary obtains a witness lease and only opens its in-process
   write gate when the granted lease epoch equals its local `authority_epoch`. The gate is closed
   on startup until that check succeeds, so a restart never serves a stale authority.
2. **Lease loss closes the gate first.** If a renew or state read fails, the gate closes
   immediately — before the lease would expire — and no new command is admitted. The database
   fence is then attempted; the fence flag, not connection termination, is authoritative.
3. **Renewal is not optional.** The controller refreshes the lease at
   `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_RENEW_INTERVAL_MS` and treats the last successful grant as a local deadline
   with a safety margin. Missing the deadline is a fence, not a warning.
4. **Promotion requires positive evidence.** A standby promotes only when all of the following
   hold: the lease is free (expired, or explicitly released), its replay LSN covers the primary's
   last reported WAL LSN, the witness recorded the primary's durability as `synchronous`, the
   service is actually in recovery, and the witness grants an epoch exactly one higher.
5. **Promotion is one-way.** `service_failover_promote` advances `authority_epoch` by exactly one,
   refuses an epoch not newer than the recorded fence epoch, revokes every session, clears the
   fence and writes an audit record, in one transaction. There is deliberately no unfence or
   resume function: a fenced or stale host can only return as a re-enrolled standby.
6. **Ambiguity fails closed.** An epoch gap, a witness epoch ahead of the database, a witness that
   cannot be reached, a writable database under a standby configuration, or an unreadable local
   state all close the gate rather than guessing.

### Bounded residual window

A command that has already been admitted on the old primary when the fence lands can still commit
if its transaction is in flight. Two configuration rules bound that window:

- `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_LEASE_TTL_MS` must exceed `BAR_LOCAL_DB_STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_MS` plus the
  renewal margin, so no admitted command can outlive the lease.
- The standby does not promote until the lease has expired, so the promotion cannot begin while
  the old primary could still be mid-commit.

This is a bounded window, not an absolute guarantee against an arbitrarily delayed network write.
It is the reason the durability evidence (point 4) is required: if the old primary was writing
without synchronous replication, its last acknowledged sales might not exist on the standby, and
the standby refuses to promote instead of silently dropping them.

### Why durability is reported by the primary

A standby can read `synchronous_commit` and `synchronous_standby_names` on its own cluster, but
that says nothing about how the *primary* acknowledged sales. The primary therefore reports
`synchronous` or `single_host` in every witness `acquire`/`renew`, and the witness stores it as
`holder_durability`. The standby requires `synchronous`; `single_host` or unrecorded durability is
a blocker (`primary_durability_not_synchronous`, `primary_durability_unrecorded`), not a warning.

After a promotion there is no standby until one is re-enrolled. The promoted host deliberately
downgrades to `single_host` durability and keeps the gate closed if `synchronous_commit = on` is
still configured with no caught-up standby, because every commit would otherwise wait for a WAL
sender that cannot exist.

## Service and witness protocol

Witness routes (all require a signed request):

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/witness/health` | Liveness only, unsigned, no state |
| `GET /v1/witness/state` | Current epoch, holder, expiry, holder LSN and durability |
| `POST /v1/witness/lease/acquire` | Take or refresh a lease (same holder keeps its epoch; a different holder gets `epoch + 1` and cannot take a live lease) |
| `POST /v1/witness/lease/renew` | Refresh; a stale epoch or a different holder is refused |
| `POST /v1/witness/lease/release` | Voluntary release on planned shutdown |

Signing covers method, path, body and a timestamp + nonce (`x-bar-witness-*` headers) with
HMAC-SHA256; the witness rejects a skewed timestamp and a replayed nonce. The shared key is
`BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_KEY` on the service and `BAR_LOCAL_WITNESS_KEY` on the witness and must match.

Witness state is written with `fsync`, an atomic rename and a `.bak` copy. On startup:

- state file present and valid → continue from its epoch;
- state file missing but backup present → **refuse to start** (never reset the epoch to 0);
- state file corrupt or from another cluster → refuse to start.

Losing the witness state is therefore an incident, not a silent reset. Restoring an older copy is
not permitted because it could reissue a used epoch.

## Configuration

Service (`BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_*`):

| Variable | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_MODE` | `disabled` | `disabled` keeps the current single-authority behaviour and the gate open; `witness` enables the gate |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_ROLE` | derived from replication role | `primary` or `standby` |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_CLUSTER_ID` | – | required in `witness` mode, 3–63 characters |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_INSTANCE_ID` | – | required in `witness` mode, unique per host |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_WITNESS_URL` | – | required in `witness` mode, loopback/private HTTP(S), no embedded credentials |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_KEY` / `_KEY_FILE` | – | required in `witness` mode, at least 32 characters, must equal the witness key |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_AUTHORITY_URL` | – | the stable address tills should use; recorded on the witness lease and shown in admin status |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_LEASE_TTL_MS` | `30000` | must exceed the statement timeout plus margin |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_RENEW_INTERVAL_MS` | `5000` | must be less than half the lease |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_POLL_INTERVAL_MS` | `5000` | standby polling |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_CATCHUP_TIMEOUT_MS` | `60000` | promotion catch-up wait |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_PROMOTE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `60` | `pg_promote()` wait |
| `BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS` | `5000` | witness request timeout |

A standby in `witness` mode also requires `BAR_LOCAL_ADMIN_DATABASE_URL` (the administrative
connection used to call `pg_promote()`), and the role must be permitted to do so.

Witness (`BAR_LOCAL_WITNESS_*`): `_CLUSTER_ID`, `_KEY`/`_KEY_FILE`, `_STATE_FILE` (default
`.local/witness/state.json`), `_HTTP_HOST`/`_HTTP_PORT` (default `127.0.0.1:8790`),
`_TLS_KEY_FILE`/`_TLS_CERT_FILE` (both or neither; required for a non-loopback bind),
`_LEASE_MAX_MS`, `_BODY_LIMIT_BYTES`, `_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS`. The witness reads no Bar database,
backup or cloud-sync settings.

## Administration surface

- `GET /v1/admin/failover` — mode, role, instance/cluster, redacted witness origin, gate
  (`open`, `reason`), lease (`held`, `epoch`, `holder_id`, `holder_matches`, `expires_at`,
  `remaining_ms`), the observed witness snapshot, local fence state, reported durability, last
  success/error and last decision.
- `POST /v1/admin/failover/fence` — loopback only, requires `settings.manage`, body
  `{ "confirm": "FENCE", "reason": "OPERATOR_FENCE" }` with an upper-case reason code. It writes an
  intent audit record first, fences, then records the outcome, and returns
  `client_repoint_required: true` plus recovery text. An audit failure is reported
  (`audit_recorded: false`) rather than hidden.
- `GET /v1/admin/status` — adds a `failover` block, and `network.capabilities.automatic_failover`
  reports `verified` only when the controller has live evidence. `replication.automatic_failover`
  is overlaid with the same evidence.
- `GET /v1/health/ready` — returns `fenced: true` and the `authority_fenced` blocker when the
  authority is fenced.
- `POST /v1/commands` and `POST /v1/approvals` — refuse with `AUTHORITY_FENCED` (503, not retryable)
  while the gate is closed.

Cashiers see a plain "trading is stopped, keep the original sale and call the owner" message; the
technical detail stays in administration.

## Windows runbook (witness)

This is a development runbook, not an installer. The witness must not run on either Bar server.

1. Choose a third machine that is on the same power/network failure domain as neither Bar server
   and that does not run PostgreSQL. Loopback is only appropriate for a single-PC trial.
2. Generate a shared key of at least 32 characters and place it on the witness and both Bar
   servers through the protected environment (not in the repository, logs or admin responses).
3. Protect the state directory (`.local/witness` by default, or `BAR_LOCAL_WITNESS_STATE_FILE`) so
   that the service account can write it and ordinary users cannot. Do not place it on a network
   share, a removable drive or a machine that will be reimaged without a controlled restore.
4. Run `npm --prefix services/bar-local run witness` from the built service directory under a
   dedicated service account, with the `BAR_LOCAL_WITNESS_*` values in its protected environment.
   Use `BAR_LOCAL_WITNESS_TLS_CERT_FILE`/`_KEY_FILE` if the bind address is not loopback.
5. Run exactly **one** witness instance for a cluster. Two witnesses with the same cluster id are
   two arbiters and void the safety argument.
6. Register the witness to start automatically before the Bar services (a Windows Scheduled Task
   "at startup" with restart-on-failure, or the same service mechanism used for the Bar service).
   A witness that is down stops trading: the primary closes its gate when it cannot renew.
7. Back up the witness state file with the same seriousness as the database: losing it stops the
   cluster from starting. An older state file must never be restored to force a new epoch.
8. To retire or replace the witness, stop trading, transfer the current state file to the new host
   while everything is stopped, and restart. Do not regenerate the state.

The witness is not yet installed by `packaging/windows/install-server.ps1`; adding witness
parameters, a scheduled task and a state-directory ACL is part of the deferred installer gate.

## Operator procedure after a failover

1. Tills keep retrying the old address and will show "trading stopped" until they are pointed at
   the promoted host. **Client auto-repointing is not implemented**; use a stable authority
   address (`BAR_LOCAL_FAILOVER_AUTHORITY_URL`) or repoint manually.
2. Do not unfence the old primary. There is no unfence function, and restarting it will not make
   it writable: its epoch no longer matches and it is fenced.
3. Re-enrol the old host as a standby from the promoted primary (the existing
   `enroll-standby.ps1`/replication tooling) before expecting synchronous durability again.
4. The promoted host runs with `single_host` durability until a standby is caught up. If
   synchronous settings remain configured without a caught-up standby, the gate stays closed and
   admin status says `durability_not_usable` rather than hanging every sale.
5. Investigate why the primary stopped: power, disk, PostgreSQL, the service process, or the
   witness itself. A witness outage alone fences trading with no promotion, which is the intended
   fail-closed result.

Manual switchover (`POST /v1/admin/replication/switchover`) is unchanged and still available for a
planned move. Witness failover does not replace it.

## Limitations and what remains unverified

- **Client repointing is manual.** No discovery, no automatic redirect, no DNS trick in this pass.
- **A witness outage stops trading.** That is deliberate, but it means the witness is a
  availability dependency; a lost witness is a service outage, not a degraded mode.
- **The witness is a single arbitral process with a local state file.** It is durable across
  restarts, not replicated. Protecting and backing up that file is an operator responsibility.
- **No split-brain proof under real partitions.** The bounded-window argument and the epoch rules
  were exercised by unit and disposable-database tests, not by physically severing two PCs.
- **`pg_promote()` privilege.** The standby must use `BAR_LOCAL_ADMIN_DATABASE_URL` provisioned for
  that purpose; packaging and protecting that credential is part of the deferred installer gate.
- **Installer/service lifecycle** for a third witness machine, firewall rules, TLS on the witness
  port and uninstall-preserves-state behaviour are not implemented.
- **Windows 7 Legacy clients, peripherals, power loss, soak and six-till topology** are deferred.
- **No hosted path.** Nothing in this change contacts Supabase.

### What the tests actually prove

- Witness: epoch advances once per holder change and never for a refresh; a live lease cannot be
  stolen; stale renew and wrong-holder renew are refused; corrupt/missing/foreign state fails
  closed; HMAC tampering, replay and clock skew are refused; a new holder does not inherit the
  previous holder's LSN.
- Controller: gate starts closed and opens only at the matching epoch; a witness outage closes the
  gate before the lease deadline and fences after it; another holder's lease fences this host; a
  witness epoch ahead of the database fences without bumping the database; a standby waits for
  catch-up and for synchronous durability evidence; a promotion bumps exactly one epoch and records
  the replay/primary LSN and durability; an epoch gap fences instead of promoting; an operator
  fence closes the gate and rejects a malformed reason.
- Database: fencing stops `current_session`-scoped commands and `allocate_sequence`; readiness
  reports `authority_fenced`; promotion advances exactly one epoch, revokes every session, clears
  the fence, audits twice across two promotions, and the pre-promotion epoch is unusable.
- HTTP: the live service exposes witness-verified `automatic_failover`, the audited fence route
  requires confirmation and an upper-case reason, and a fenced service refuses trading.

### What the tests do not prove

No physical two-PC failover, no real network partition, no power loss, no Windows service
lifecycle, no witness restart under load, no six-till soak, no Windows 7 client, no installer and no
customer cutover. The bounded in-flight window is argued from configuration and timing, not observed
under a forced partition.
