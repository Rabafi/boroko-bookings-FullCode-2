# Tsa Bonno marketing — ecosystem story and design direction

23 October 2026. Records the user's direction following the combined audit and rendered visual review. This is a concrete content/design brief; the website has not yet been changed.

## The positioning

Sell **Tsa Bonno HospitalityOS as a hospitality ecosystem** first. Then present its applications in this order everywhere the product family is introduced:

1. **LodgingOS** — flagship; the first and most developed product story.
2. **Restaurant & Bar POS** — the second product, with clear Restaurant and Bar operating paths.
3. **HotelOS — Coming soon** — a compelling preview, with trial/download/purchase/activation temporarily unavailable.

The reason to choose Tsa Bonno should be tangible: staff can run daily work, guests can reach the business through relevant web experiences, and owners can see operations and financial performance through the appropriate mobile/reporting tools. Explain these connected workflows with real product evidence.

The ecosystem is a product family with separate applications and commercial boundaries. Do not imply that buying one product automatically licenses all three, that separate businesses automatically share all data, or that every capability is included in every tier.

## Suggested first-screen copy

**Eyebrow:** Tsa Bonno HospitalityOS

**Headline:** Run the day. Know the business. Stay in control.

**Supporting copy:** Hospitality software for the people serving your guests and the people growing your business. Bring bookings or service, money, stock and owner visibility into a clearer working day—with dedicated apps for lodges, restaurants and bars. HotelOS is coming soon.

**Primary action:** Start your free month

**Secondary action:** See the ecosystem

**Trial line:** 1-month free trial. No credit card required.

**Visible feature strip:** Manager mobile app · Direct booking and guest web experiences · Reports and financial control

The primary action opens a clearly labelled product chooser, in the required order, rather than silently selecting Lodge. LodgingOS and Restaurant/Bar use their matching eligible trial flows. Hotel offers Register interest. Explain the once-per-property 30-day policy and relevant limits in the trial detail; do not claim unlimited usage.

The hero should show real, readable app proof: a focused desktop workflow, the Manager phone view, and a relevant guest web screen. Use separate frames or a disciplined composition with correct aspect ratios. The trial and main product choice remain visible on phone. Make web/mobile features prominent in the first screen and explain eligibility beside the detailed benefit sections.

## Landing-page story

### 1. Show the ecosystem working for a real business

**Suggested heading:** Your team, your guests and your business—better connected.

Explain three perspectives with a short example, rather than repeating a catalogue:

- **Your team:** see what needs doing and record daily work in the appropriate desktop app.
- **Your guests:** discover availability, send booking requests or use the applicable guest web experience.
- **You:** check authorised operational information from your phone and use reports to make decisions.

Illustrate a supported journey: a direct booking request reaches the lodge workflow; reception prepares the stay; room and outlet activity contribute to the appropriate records; management reviews confirmed results. Use timestamps, package labels and pending/synced status where necessary. Do not animate a fabricated cross-app integration.

### 2. Lead with LodgingOS

**Suggested headline:** A better-run property, from the first enquiry to the end-of-day review.

Build its story around outcomes:

- **Win direct demand:** show the branded booking site and a guest request reaching reception.
- **Give the front desk clarity:** arrivals, room readiness, guest details, invoices and balances.
- **Control more of the property:** show housekeeping, expenses, supplies, POS or stock where the selected package includes them.
- **Keep the owner informed:** show the Manager phone view and a readable report, rather than another generic desktop thumbnail.

Lead with the best supported product experience, then make the package ladder easy to understand: Starter for the front desk foundation, Standard for deeper management/reporting, Pro for mobile oversight, direct booking and broader commercial operations. A feature can be aspirational in the story without being falsely presented as included in Starter.

**Actions:** Explore LodgingOS · Start your free month

### 3. Follow with Restaurant & Bar POS

**Suggested headline:** Keep service moving. Keep stock and cash in view.

Tell the shift story: open the shift/till, take the order, route service where applicable, track stock and exceptions, close with a clear record, then review performance. Show actual current terminal and management screens.

Make **Restaurant** and **Bar** choices explicit within this product story. Restaurant can demonstrate floor/kitchen service and production; Bar leads with counter sales, tabs, pack stock, staff and cash close. Neither path should inherit Lodge onboarding language.

Elevate accounting and reporting as reasons to consider the product: understanding sales, costs, purchasing and financial records is part of running the business. Clearly identify package/add-on and activation requirements. Restaurant Control/Growth include the accounting feature family in the catalogue; Bar Accounting & Workforce is an optional bundle requiring Stock & Purchasing Pro. Do not imply that Bar Base includes full accounting or payroll.

**Actions:** Explore Restaurant & Bar · Start your free month

### 4. Introduce HotelOS last, as Coming soon

**Suggested headline:** A clearer hotel day, from arrivals to night audit.

Make this a confident preview using genuine HotelOS imagery: today's arrivals/departures, a guest folio, room readiness and housekeeping, rates/corporate business and close. Describe the operational benefit of each. Core, optional and future capabilities must remain distinguishable.

Use a visible Coming soon badge. Keep the page attractive and discoverable; disabled availability must not make the page look abandoned. Replace transactional actions with **Explore HotelOS** and **Register interest** or **Request a demo**. A demo action should state whether it requests a conversation or a scheduled preview; it must not pretend to be immediate access.

### 5. Make the ecosystem's strongest benefits a buying argument

**Suggested heading:** See beyond the front desk or the till.

Use three substantial, evidence-led stories:

| Story | Buyer benefit | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **Your business on your phone** | Less dependence on manual owner updates; easier authorised oversight | Real product-aware Manager views, with a concise explanation of what can be viewed or acted on |
| **A web experience for your guests** | A professional direct route into the business | Branded booking pages and actual guest journeys; Hotel guest portal remains part of the Coming soon/optional Hotel story |
| **Numbers you can act on** | Understand collections, outstanding balances, expenses, stock and performance | Readable report examples; relevant accounting workflows with product/package scope |

Each story needs a recognisable operational problem, a real screen, a specific improvement and a next action. Avoid repeated small feature cards with unreadable whole-screen images.

### 6. Make starting feel concrete

**Suggested heading:** Give it a month in your business.

**Suggested copy:** Try the available Tsa Bonno app with your own setup. Let your team work through the daily routine, explore the reports your package offers, and see how the system fits your operation before choosing a paid package.

Show three steps: choose your app, set up your business, try your daily workflows. Provide visible setup help and transparent package prices. Explain any activation/connectivity requirements accurately. Do not promise an unverified setup time or include Hotel in available-now trials.

Repeat the product-aware **Start your free month** action after the core stories and at the page end. Show no-credit-card wording only where the actual flow supports it. Pricing, limits and what happens after the trial must be easy to find.

## App-shell colours define each product page

Use a shared typography, spacing, card and accessibility system, with product-specific palettes drawn from current shell source. The ecosystem landing page can use a restrained neutral foundation and introduce each product through its own accents. Do not give every product the Lodge green template.

| Surface | Actual shell palette to carry through | Visual treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **LodgingOS** | Forest accent #174c3a; rail #0f3d2c to #0c2d23; warm paper #f4efe7 | Calm, clear property control; forest navigation/accents, light readable proof areas |
| **Restaurant & Bar POS** | Coral #c95635; dark plum rail #2f222a to #211920; warm cream surfaces | Service energy with operational clarity; coral actions and plum framing, including the Bar profile |

Colour matching is a palette direction, not a requirement to copy an app's dense layout into the website. Adapt colour roles for readable marketing text and accessible buttons; app-shell swatches are not automatically suitable for white text. Restaurant and Bar should feel like related profiles of the same app rather than unrelated brands.

## How the story earns the sale

- Show the cost of unclear work through relatable examples—missed follow-up, uncertain balances, stock questions, owners waiting for updates—without inventing savings figures.
- Demonstrate the workflow and result with real, crisp screenshots and useful captions.
- Give mobile, guest web and financial visibility enough space to become reasons to buy, rather than footer extras.
- Explain package value and provide a meaningful one-month trial path for available products.
- Use real customer evidence only when permission and attribution are established. Until then, label sample data and demonstrations honestly.
- Describe concrete strengths rather than claiming competitors lack features, universal integrations, tax certification, unlimited offline operation or guaranteed financial outcomes.

The intended distinction is the way the relevant team, guest-facing tools and owner controls fit around a hospitality business's working day. The website must show that value clearly enough for a buyer to picture using it.

## Acceptance criteria

The revised direction is met when the first screen communicates the ecosystem and trial; all product introductions follow Lodge → Restaurant & Bar → Hotel Coming soon; page palettes match the app shells; mobile/web/reporting/accounting benefits are prominent and correctly scoped; screenshots are readable and proportional; each page has a persuasive outcome-led story; and every action leads to the matching product and honest availability state.

Implementation must also address the existing audit's broken conversion paths, visual defects, accessibility and error recovery. This brief does not replace those requirements. No website code, app behavior, customer manuals or release state changed when this document was written.
