Stock Control

How to reduce waste in your lodge bar and kitchen.

Waste rarely announces itself loudly. It usually shows up as repeated shortages, unexplained restocking, inconsistent margins, and the uneasy feeling that the stock story never fully adds up.

Start by making stock movement more visible

If purchases, usage, and remaining stock are tracked in separate habits, losses become easy to miss. The first step is not perfection. It is visibility.

Connect sales to stock where possible

When bar and kitchen sales are recorded in a connected POS workflow, it becomes easier to compare what was sold against what should still be on hand.

Watch for repeated quiet patterns

Common warning signs include items that run out earlier than expected, frequent urgent purchases, and categories where staff cannot confidently explain the remaining stock position.

Make counting part of the operating rhythm

Regular stock checks, even simple ones, reduce the chance that waste builds for too long without being noticed. The goal is steady control, not occasional panic.

Owner visibility matters here too

Stock waste is not just a kitchen issue. It is a management issue. Owners need a clearer view of what is moving, what is leaking, and where commercial discipline needs to improve.

Better stock control supports growth

As soon as the lodge has a busier bar, kitchen, or room-supplies workflow, stronger stock tracking stops being optional. It becomes part of protecting margin and keeping operations disciplined.
